Free Music Practice Log Template (Printable PDF)

By Jisoo Kim · Updated Oct 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer

Download a one-page printable practice log with columns for the date, the piece or exercise, minutes actually played, one thing that worked and one thing to fix, plus a weekly review box. It works for any instrument and takes about a minute after each session.

Key takeaways
  • One page, one line per session, one review box per week.
  • People stop logging because it feels tedious and because nothing big seems to change.
  • A log is not for recording big changes — it shows how steadily you practiced and how much it takes before things change.

What's in the template?

The template is a single A4 page with one line per session and a review box for the week:

ColumnWhat to write
DateThe day you practiced
Piece / exerciseWhat you worked on, and the kind of practice (scales, section work, run-through…)
Minutes playedTime you were actually playing, not time at the instrument
What workedOne thing that went better today
What to fixOne thing to work on next time
Weekly reviewAt the end of the week: what changed, and one thing to try next week

Download it as a printable page or a PDF.

How do you fill it in?

Right after practice, take one minute and fill in one line. Keep it short: a piece name, a number of minutes, and a few words in each of the last two columns. At the end of the week, read the lines and write one sentence in the review box.

Write the minutes soon after the session. Memory of practice time drifts: in a 30-year follow-up study, students' later estimates of their practice did not match the daily records they had kept.

Why do people stop keeping a practice log?

Two reasons come up most. The first is that it is tedious: one more thing to do when practice is over. The second is that nothing big seems to change from day to day, so the lines start to feel pointless.

What is a practice log actually for?

A practice log is not a place to record big changes. It is a steady record of how consistently you practiced, so that you learn, for yourself, how much practice it takes before things change. Small, similar lines are exactly what it is supposed to look like. Read four weeks of them together and the change becomes visible.

For a fuller version with practice types, to-do lists and methods, see how to keep a music practice journal.

Writing minutes down by hand is the part most people skip. MU:note counts only the time you actually play, so that column fills itself.

Don't want to fill in the minutes by hand?

The minutes column is the one people skip most. MU:note fills it in for you: it hears the instrument you set and counts only the time you actually play, leaving out silences longer than 15 seconds. You can keep the paper log for notes, or keep everything in the app.

Frequently asked questions

Is the template free?

Yes. You can print it, copy it and share it with students.

Does it work for any instrument?

Yes. The columns are the same for piano, strings, winds, voice or guitar.

How often should I review it?

Once a week. Read the week's lines and write one thing to change next week in the review box.

Let the minutes write themselves

MU:note listens and counts only the time you actually play. Audio is never recorded.
