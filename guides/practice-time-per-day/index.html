How Long Should You Practice Your Instrument Each Day?

By Jisoo Kim · Updated Oct 2026 · 6 min read
Quick answer

There is no single right number. Hobby players get more from 10 minutes every day than from one hour once a week, students preparing for entrance exams alongside school can aim for around five hours a day, and music majors do best with a steady routine of their own. Whatever your level, stretch your focus a little at a time and plan your breaks instead of playing on without focus.

Key takeaways
  • How you practice matters more than how long: accuracy during practice predicted next-day results better than practice time did.
  • Short daily sessions beat one long weekly session, partly because memory consolidates between days.
  • Lengthen your focus gradually, schedule breaks, and stretch between blocks.

Is there a right number of hours?

No single number works for everyone, and hours alone do not predict progress. In a 2009 study, 17 graduate and advanced undergraduate piano majors practiced a difficult three-bar passage from a Shostakovich concerto until they felt ready, then played it again the next day. Practice time and the number of repetitions were not related to how well they played the next day. What mattered was how often they played the passage correctly during practice.

So start from how you practice, then decide how long.

Is 10 minutes every day better than an hour once a week?

Yes, for most hobby players. Many people practice in bursts on the days they feel motivated and then skip several days. Ten minutes every day keeps the work going and gives your memory time to settle between sessions.

A small laboratory study points the same way. Twenty-nine musicians who were not pianists learned a short nine-note sequence on a keyboard in three sessions spaced 5 minutes, 6 hours or 24 hours apart. Only the group with a full day, and a night's sleep, between the first two sessions became more accurate in the second session.

How do exam students fit practice around school?

Students preparing for music entrance exams often struggle with how to divide the day between practice and schoolwork. If you are also studying, around five hours of practice a day is a reasonable baseline. Split it into blocks — for example a morning block before school and two blocks in the evening — and decide in advance what each block is for, so the hours do not blur together.

How long do people actually practice in one sitting?

32 minutes
is the median length of a practice session in MU:note from start to finish (piano 37, strings 28, winds 29). Based on 9,495 practice sessions recorded with MU:note in the 90 days to October 18, 2026.

That is shorter than many people picture when they think of a practice day, and it is a useful reality check: a long day is usually several sessions, not one.

How long should one session be, and when should you rest?

Long, unfocused practice is less useful than shorter, focused blocks with planned breaks. Notice how long you can stay focused today, then stretch it a little at a time. Decide your break interval in advance instead of waiting until you are tired, and use part of each break for a short stretch — it helps your posture and your concentration.

Frequently asked questions

Is 10 minutes a day enough?

For a hobby player, 10 focused minutes every day helps more than one long session a week. Add time once the daily habit holds.

How many hours do entrance-exam students practice?

If you are also keeping up with school, around five hours a day is a workable baseline, split into blocks with breaks.

Does this apply to guitar and voice too?

Yes. The same principles apply to piano, strings, winds, voice and guitar (acoustic and classical).

Should I stop when I lose focus?

Take a planned break instead of playing on without focus. A short stretch helps both posture and concentration.
